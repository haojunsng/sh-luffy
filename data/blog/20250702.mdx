## **Introduction & Motivation**

I finally got to building my first personal portfolio website with `Next.js` and hosted on `Cloudflare Pages`.

Edwin endorsed `Next.js` - it was modern, flexible, and with enough documentation.

Why `Cloudflare Pages`? Because my site is static (for now), and I wanted something fast, free, and dead simple to deploy. Also, I’m a sucker for a seamless developer experiences, and Cloudflare’s “just push to main” workflow is _chef’s kiss_. (_I also like that they have a preview environment on PR branches, unlike `Render`..._)

If I ever need to add dynamic features, I can always migrate over to Cloudflare Workers or move to a more full-featured host. But for now, Pages is more than enough.

## **Technical Challenges**

- **CSS & Styling**: I thought Tailwind would save me from CSS hell. Actually, it mostly did. But then came the SVGs, the mobile breakpoints, figuring out how to calculate offsets and how much to scale... thinking back - I'm glad I chose to specialise in DE.

- **Image Optimization**: Next.js’s `<Image />` component is great, but figuring out how to make images look the way they should on all devices took way too much time and energy out of me.

- **Build & Deploy**: This wasn't something foreign - and Cloudflare Pages made deployment really easy too. From deploying to production to setting a custom domain, it was really seamless.

## **What I Learned**

- **Modern frameworks are powerful, but you still need to read the docs.** Next.js is awesome, but it’s not magic. Understanding the basics (routing, data fetching, static vs. dynamic) is key, and _good old junior dev (AI)_ really helped me structure the unknowns and provided me with concise and easily understandable explanations.
- **CSS is still the final boss.** Tailwind helps, but even now I still find it a struggle to make it work on my first few tries. I guess this is something that comes with more practice.
- **Cloudflare Pages is great for static sites.** The developer experience is smooth, and the free tier is generous. (_No credit card details required!_)

## **Final Thoughts**

Building this site was equal parts fun, frustrating, and rewarding. I learned a ton, shipped something I’m happy with, and now have a home for all my projects and writing.

Stay tuned for more posts as I continue to grow my collection of personal projects!
